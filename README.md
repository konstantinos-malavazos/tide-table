# Tide Table

A coastline-prediction game prototype. You survey a hidden coastline with a limited number of anchors, reconstruct it from your measurements, and get scored on how close you land.

> **Status:** early prototype and concept sketch. This is **not** a finished or final design; it needs refinement. See [Future work](#future-work).

> **Attribution:** the idea and all code in this repository were generated by an AI assistant (Logos, an OpenClaw agent running the DeepSeek V4 Pro model, `deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro`), at the repository owner's request. It is the assistant's work, not the owner's.

## The idea

You are given a hidden coastline under a fog of war. Somewhere in the grid is a shoreline (water on one side, land on the other) plus a couple of planted surprises: a lake sitting in the middle of dry land, and a headland jutting out to sea. You cannot see any of it.

Your only tool is a limited set of survey anchors. Dropping an anchor on a cell takes a measurement and reveals whether that cell is water, sand, or land. Each anchor costs you points at scoring time, so there is a real tension: sample densely along the coast to nail its shape, or hold anchors in reserve to probe for hidden anomalies.

Once you have placed your anchors, you predict the whole coastline. The model reconstructs the most plausible shoreline that passes through your measurements. Then the truth is revealed and you are scored on how close your prediction came.

The anomalies exist specifically because a smooth coastline model cannot see them coming. The only way to catch a lake or a headland is to spend an anchor measuring it directly.

## How to play

Open `index.html` in any browser. No build step, no dependencies.

- Click cells to drop survey anchors (up to 12). Click a cell again to remove its anchor. The predicted coast redraws live as you survey.
- **Reveal truth** shows the real coast and scores you. Red outlines mark cells you got wrong.
- **New coastline** generates a fresh map with 2 hidden anomalies.

## Mechanics (under the hood)

- Grid is 30 x 16. Three tile types: water, sand, land.
- The hidden truth is generated from a sea-level curve (a sum of sines) thresholded into tiles, then 2 anomalies are planted (a lake and a headland).
- Each anchor reveals the true tile at its cell. From that tile the model estimates a sea level at that column: water -> `row - 1.3`, sand -> `row`, land -> `row + 1.3`.
- Prediction fits a smooth sea-level curve through the anchor estimates (Gaussian-weighted by horizontal distance, relaxing to a flat middle where there are no anchors), then thresholds it back into tiles. Anchored cells are pinned to their true tile.

## Scoring

- Only cells within 1 cell of any sand (the coastline band) count. Deep water and deep land are trivial.
- Exact match = full, off-by-one-tier (water/sand or sand/land) = 0.4, wrong side (water/land) = 0.
- That fraction is multiplied by an anchor-efficiency bonus: `1 + (12 - anchorsUsed) * 0.06`.
- Final points = coastline% x bonus x 1000.

## Design notes

The first pass used literal wave-function-collapse for the predictor. WFC's local-adjacency model cannot reconstruct a wavy coastline from sparse anchors: it emits blocky noise, and anchors barely moved the score. It was replaced with smooth interpolation, which reads clean and keeps the anchor -> collapse -> reveal -> score loop intact. WFC is still planned for the anomaly-generation layer (procedurally planting features that break the smooth model).
